## What "Gloss Bomb" actually covers

Gloss Bomb is Fenty's lip category, not a single product. The line launched as one universal luminizer and grew into a family that now spans four different textures plus limited editions and sets.

### Gloss Bomb Universal Lip Luminizer

The original. The formula is built on shea butter and delivers a sheer wash of tint with visible shimmer and a wet-looking finish. Sephora's product copy describes an XXL doe-foot wand designed to cover the lip in one swipe, a non-sticky feel, and the peach-vanilla scent the line is known for. It comes in 12 shades at Ulta and six core shades on Sephora's listing.

If you have never bought a Fenty gloss and you just want the famous one, this is it, in Fenty Glow or Fu$$y.

### Gloss Bomb Heat Universal Lip Luminizer + Plumper

Same gloss architecture, plus what Fenty calls a Plump Job Complex. The ingredient list includes methyl nicotinate, capsicum frutescens fruit extract and ginger root oil, which is where the warming tingle comes from. Sephora lists it at $26 for 0.30 oz, and the brand's own consumer study of 30 volunteers reported 96% agreeing it feels nourishing and gives a high-shine finish, and 93% agreeing it feels conditioning and leaves lips looking smoother.

Worth knowing: the tingle is real and some people don't enjoy it. A Space NK staff reviewer described it as tingling "a bit but doesn't last very long," and Sephora's own instructions say to discontinue if irritation occurs. If your lips react badly to plumping ingredients, skip this one and take the original.

### Gloss Bomb Cream Color Drip Lip Cream

Zero shimmer, creamy medium-to-full coverage colour, vitamin A in the mix. It's the gloss for people who want their lips to look coloured rather than sparkly. Sephora lists it at $23 for 9 mL, and it carries a 4.7-star average across more than 2,200 reviews on Fenty's own product page — the highest review volume of any single Gloss Bomb listing that showed up in this research.

### Gloss Bomb Stix

A twist-up gloss stick rather than a liquid in a tube. Retailers list it at around $26, matching the Heat price. If you dislike the feeling of a wet gloss wand or you're applying on the move without a mirror, this is the format that solves that. Shade names overlap with the liquid line (Fenty Glow, RiRi, Hot Chocolit, Sp'ice Cold), so you can match a shade you already like.

### Mini Gloss Bomb

A 0.18 fl oz version at $16 (Ulta). It's marketed as a gift-with-purchase freebie and a travel size, and it's genuinely useful for a handbag. Just don't buy it expecting value: you're paying roughly 33% less for about 60% of the product. More on that below.

### Limited editions and sets

Fenty runs limited Gloss Bomb drops regularly. Recent examples include a four-shade **Arcane** collaboration priced at $22 per gloss per Billboard's report, with shades Risky Rulin, Rogue On Riche$, Rebel Rais'r and Kaboom, and a **Midnight Diamonds** line whose Caviar Crystalz shade was listed at CA$31.50 on Sephora Canada. There's also the **Glossy Posse Volume 7.0** trio of three full-size glosses, which Fenty's own site listed at HK$400 in Hong Kong.

Limited editions are where the good shades disappear fastest and where regional pricing gets weird. Check the price in your own market before assuming it matches the US number.

## Is the mini actually cheaper?

No, and the maths is not close.

The full size is $23 for 9 mL, or about $2.56 per mL. The mini is $16 for 0.18 fl oz, which is about 5.3 mL, or roughly $3.00 per mL. You save $7 up front and pay about 17% more per millilitre. That's a normal trade for a travel size, but it's worth stating plainly because the mini gets marketed as the affordable entry point and priced like it isn't.

The honest reason to buy the mini is that you want the shade in your bag without committing to a tube you might not finish. That's a real reason. "It's cheaper" isn't.

## Which shades are worth it, and what they look like

Fenty's shade naming is deliberately playful, which makes it hard to know what you're ordering. Here's what the well-known ones are, based on brand and retailer descriptions:

- **Fenty Glow** — shimmering rose nude. The default. It's the shade Fenty has released across all four formulas, which tells you something about which one sells.
- **Fu$$y** — sheer pink. The other perennial. The Heat version of it is the one Sephora's bestseller carousel keeps surfacing.
- **Glass Slipper** — clear with shimmer. A good gloss-for-guys or gloss-for-people-who-hate-colour pick, and the version one Space NK reviewer specifically praised for staying inside the lip line.
- **Fuchsia Flex** — deep holographic fuchsia. Heavy pigment. One reviewer described it as feeling like a complete lip look in a single product.
- **RiRi** — mauve with purple glitter. More wearable in person than the tube suggests, according to the Space NK write-up.
- **Lemon Lava** — clear with gold glitter. Almost no colour payoff, all iridescent shimmer.
- **Hot Chocolit** — a brown-toned shimmer shade, available in both the liquid and the Stix format.
- **Dragon Mami** — the newer shade bundled into the Glossy Posse Volume 7.0 trio.

The useful rule from the Space NK team's nine-shade test: the more pigmented the shade, the longer it lasted. Their lighter-shade testers were reapplying within an hour to two hours and suggested pairing those with a darker lip liner. If you want a gloss you don't have to think about, buy pigment. If you want a topper, buy the clear ones and accept the reapplication.

## What to actually buy, based on how you use gloss

**If you want the classic Fenty look:** Gloss Bomb Universal in Fenty Glow. $23, and it's the shade that made the product famous. If you're unsure and can only justify one tube, this is the lowest-risk $23 in the line.

**If you want fuller-looking lips and don't mind the tingle:** Gloss Bomb Heat. $26, plus the shea butter and vitamin E base. Budget for the possibility that you hate the sensation, because that's the one thing reviewers consistently flag in both directions.

**If you want colour, not sparkle:** Gloss Bomb Cream. $23, no shimmer, and the deepest shade range for people who want the gloss to do the work of a lipstick.

**If you hate wet-texture applicators:** Gloss Bomb Stix. Around $26. It removes the one complaint people have about liquid gloss, which is that it's liquid.

**If you're buying for someone else or testing a shade:** Mini Gloss Bomb. $16, and yes, it's worse value per mL. It's also the right call when you don't know whether they'll like the peach-vanilla scent.

**If you want three at once:** the Glossy Posse sets, which Fenty prices regionally. The volume 7.0 trio has three full-size glosses, so the per-unit maths improves, but the entry price does too.
